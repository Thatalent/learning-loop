# The Learning Ladder (a.k.a. the Learning Pyramid)

The "learning pyramid" orders learning activities from passive to active:

1. Lecture / being told
2. Reading
3. Audio-visual (diagrams, video)
4. Demonstration
5. Discussion
6. Practice by doing
7. Teaching others

**Use the ordering, not the numbers.** The pyramid is usually shown with retention percentages (5%, 10%, 20%, 30%, 50%, 75%, 90%). Researchers who have tried to trace those numbers have found no study that produced them, and they are suspiciously round. Don't quote them as fact. The underlying idea, that active, effortful, generative learning beats passive exposure, is well supported. So treat this as a ladder: climb as high as the situation allows.

## What the evidence actually supports

These are the techniques with the strongest research backing. Map them onto the ladder:

- **Retrieval practice** (testing effect): pulling information out of memory strengthens it more than re-reading. Quizzes, flashcards, "explain it back without looking."
- **Generation / prediction**: guessing before being told, even wrongly, improves retention of the correct answer.
- **Elaboration and self-explanation**: explaining why and how, in your own words.
- **Spacing**: revisiting material after a delay (a day, then several days) beats cramming.
- **Interleaving**: mixing problem types forces you to choose the right approach, which builds Analyze-level skill.
- **Dual coding**: pairing words with visuals.

## Mapping rungs to AI-assisted activities

| Rung | In a session with Claude | Typical Bloom level supported |
|---|---|---|
| Being told / reading | Claude's explanation; linked docs | Remember |
| Audio-visual | Diagram of the flow, chart of the data, annotated screenshot | Remember → Understand |
| Demonstration | Claude runs the real query or command and narrates what it's checking and why | Understand → Apply |
| Discussion | Socratic back-and-forth; the person proposes a hypothesis and Claude probes it | Understand → Analyze |
| Practice | The person runs the next query, writes the fix, or diagnoses a variant scenario themselves, with Claude as reviewer | Apply → Analyze |
| Teaching others | The person writes the explainer, runbook, or debrief; Claude critiques it | Evaluate → Create |

## Picking activities

- Start one rung above where the person is comfortable.
- Combine rungs: a diagram plus a prediction question beats either alone.
- Move to practice as soon as the person can attempt it, even imperfectly. Mistakes during practice are the most valuable learning moments, so don't pre-empt them.
- End high: if there's time for only one active thing, make it an explanation in the person's own words.
