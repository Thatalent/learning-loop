# Teaching Sequence: from zero to the goal

A lesson must work for someone who knows nothing about the topic. Activities test and strengthen understanding; they can't create it. So every objective follows **teach → show → try → explain**, and the lesson builds level on level.

Why (research summary): learners grasp new material better when they already know the main terms and parts (Mayer's *pre-training* principle), learn more from words with pictures than words alone (*multimedia*), and from short learner-paced segments (*segmenting*) with the key parts highlighted (*signaling*). In a 2025 randomized trial at Harvard, a structured AI tutor that scaffolded steps and managed cognitive load produced more than twice the learning gains of an active-learning class; a generic chatbot setup does not do this on its own. Worked examples and faded steps help novices most and can slow experts (*expertise reversal*), so the amount of teaching scales with the gap.

## 1. Know where they start (interview only if needed)

- **If learning-loop already established their level this session, skip the interview.** Reuse the edge and target it found, and say so on the title slide.
- **Otherwise interview briefly** (one message, then adjust): what they need to be able to do and why; what they already know about it, in their own words; one or two probe questions climbing from the basics, stopping where it breaks; and the words or ideas that confuse them.
- Listen for **misconceptions**, not just gaps. A wrong idea needs its own "common mistake" step; adding facts on top won't fix it.
- **Starting from zero is normal.** If they can't answer the first probe, the lesson starts at Remember with a foundations section. Never skip a level to reach the goal faster.

## 2. Build a knowledge map before the slides

List the 3–7 ideas the goal depends on, in prerequisite order (each one needs only the ones before it). Mark which ones the learner already has. The lesson teaches the missing ones, in that order, and only those. For someone who knows nothing, the map is the lesson's spine; for someone further along, it shrinks to their gap.

## 3. Each objective is a teaching segment

| Step | Slide | What it contains |
|---|---|---|
| **Teach** | Concept slide | A one-sentence idea in plain words, then 3–6 sentences explaining *what* it is, *why* it works that way, and *how* it connects to what came before. Define every new term where it first appears. |
| **Show** | Diagram slide | An inline SVG built for this idea: the parts, labeled, with arrows for flow or cause, the key part **highlighted**, and a short caption next to it (not a paragraph below). Build it up step by step if it has more than five parts. |
| **Example** | Worked example | One concrete, real case walked through step by step, with the reasoning for each step. For novices, add a second example from a different setting. |
| **Try** | Activity | The kit activity that fits (see `activity-catalog.md`), using only what has been taught so far. |
| **Explain** | Feedback + recap | The activity's explanation says *why* the answer is right and why the tempting wrong one is wrong, then one line connecting it to the next segment. |

Scale it to the learner: **novices get every step** (and a "common mistake" note); learners partway there get Teach + Show briefly, then Try; learners near the goal start at Try and see the explanation only if they miss.

## 4. Progressive build

- **Foundations first:** for a learner at Remember, open with a short "the big picture" segment: the 3–5 key terms and parts and how they relate, as one diagram, before any detail.
- **One new idea per segment.** If a slide needs two new terms to make sense, it's two segments.
- **Reuse and grow one diagram.** Start the lesson with the simplest version and add to it in later segments, so each new idea visibly attaches to the old ones.
- **Check before climbing.** A segment's activity must be solved (or retried after its explanation) before the next level. If the learner misses it twice, the next slide re-teaches it a different way (another example, a simpler diagram) instead of moving on.
- **Interleave a quick look back** every two or three segments: one question on an earlier idea (retrieval practice).

## 5. Length

Aim for 10–20 minutes. A from-zero lesson may need 15–25 slides; that's fine because most are short teaching slides. If the gap is too big for one sitting, build part 1 up to the next solid level, and offer part 2.
